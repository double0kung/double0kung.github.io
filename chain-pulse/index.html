How the reading is worked out

Blockchain activity shows how much is happening, not which way. A fee spike can be a rush to buy or a rush to exit. So the score combines direction with intensity:

  • Price momentum (45% of direction): 24-hour and 7-day price change.
  • Order flow (35%): share of traded volume where the buyer crossed the spread (taker buys). Above 50% means buyers are the aggressors.
  • Exchange wallets (20%): net change in the balances of known exchange wallets, over 24 hours where history is available and from the live feed otherwise. Coins moving in are typically headed for sale; coins moving out, for holding.
  • Live exchange flow: the same wallets re-checked every 15 seconds (ETH) or 60 seconds (BTC, including unconfirmed transactions). Every balance change shows as an IN or OUT entry. The feed builds while this page is open and is kept for 24 hours in this browser.
  • Network activity (intensity): fees, backlog, block fullness and transaction counts. A busy chain amplifies the direction (up to 1.4×); a quiet one damps it (down to 0.6×).

Score runs −100 (sell-off) to +100 (demand). Above +40 strong demand, +15 to +40 demand building, −15 to +15 neutral, −15 to −40 selling pressure, below −40 sell-off.

Price outlook. For each time horizon the page finds the past hours (about 41 days of hourly data) that looked most like the current one, by 6-hour and 24-hour momentum and order flow, and counts how often price was higher afterwards. The ranges come from how far price actually moved over that horizon, scaled to current volatility. The method is replayed on its own history and its hit rate is shown, so you can see how much to trust it. Short-term direction is hard to predict: expect figures close to 50%.

Limits. The exchange wallet list is a sample of publicly labelled wallets, not total exchange flow, and exchanges reshuffle wallets internally (a hot-to-cold move between two watched wallets nets to zero). Full exchange-flow data needs a paid provider (Glassnode, CryptoQuant). This is a heuristic indicator, not financial advice.
